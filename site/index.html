Why it exists

Life changes fast. Paperwork doesn't keep up.

Family caregivers juggle calls, visits, emails, portals and letters across offices that don't talk to each other. When something doesn't add up, the answer is usually already somewhere in your own phone. It's just scattered. phone-net pulls it into one timeline you can search, so you can ask clear questions in writing.

What we learned doing it

Getting complex systems to talk, one plain step at a time

Worked out the hard way, by a family caregiver, on very little sleep.

  1. Gather everything you already own. Phone audio, SD cards, email, scanned letters and bills. Pull only what's new; verify every copy.
  2. Fix the dates. Backups stamp files with the day they were copied. The audio itself usually knows when it was recorded.
  3. Hear each voice. Level quiet speech, transcribe, and separate who's talking, so the TV isn't mistaken for a person.
  4. Search by word and by meaning. Your own filenames are an index too, misspellings and all.
  5. Put what changed next to what didn't. What happened on one line; what was promised and paid on the other.
  6. Check the pattern before you believe it. One exciting spike turned out to be TV and copy-day dates. Say so.
  7. Include your own worst moments. A record that only shows one side isn't a record.
  8. Ask in writing, one question per office. Certified mail, a deadline, then the free advocate. Fewer phone calls, fewer triggers.
  9. Pace the machine and yourself. Queues that wait for free resources, a pause switch, handoff notes. No marathons.

Rules it runs on

Local, honest, and yours to share

  • Only your own devices. No one else's accounts or systems.
  • Local by default. Nothing uploads, emails or publishes by itself.
  • Honest by design. Speaker names are marked as automatic, low-confidence lines are flagged, and your own raised voice stays in.
  • Built with free tools. WhisperX, pyannote, Ollama, ffmpeg, adb, open models.
  • Check your state's recording law. This is a tool, not legal advice.
